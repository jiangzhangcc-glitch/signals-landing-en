🔗 Traceability · Are the signals real?

The AI world produces enormous information daily, but most of it gets distorted through secondhand retelling and clickbait. Signals API's first principle: every signal leads back to its source. We pull from GitHub repository pages, HuggingFace model cards, original YouTube videos and Silicon Valley feeds — each signal carries its original URL, publish time and publisher. Click once and you're at the source, with no middleman interpretation. Why does this matter? In a field with extreme information distortion, "who said it" and "where it came from" are the anchor of value. Use publish time to judge freshness, publisher to judge credibility, and the original link for deeper research. For researchers, traceability means every citation is verifiable. For investors, it means decisions withstand scrutiny. For creators, it means you can cite with confidence. We don't produce opinions — we deliver verifiable facts, with "back to the source" as the default behavior of the product.

⏳ Time compression · What happened in 24 hours?

Say you want to know what happened in AI today: hundreds of new repos on GitHub, dozens of new models on HuggingFace, hundreds of AI videos on YouTube, and a flood of Valley news. Manually scanning that takes half a day. Signals API compresses it into one list: four fixed collections a day, automatic deduplication and classification, condensing four streams into a few dozen key signals. Further compression comes from filtering — by company (OpenAI, DeepSeek, Qwen…), by topic (model, agent, security, funding…), by type. Time compression isn't about seeing less; it's about seeing less of what doesn't matter — turning an eight-hour information hunt into a five-minute read. For professionals whose time is money, this compression is the product. No more maintaining dozens of bookmarks and RSS feeds — one signal dashboard is the single entry point.

🗂️ History archive · Where does the timeline live?

Most information products only give you "now". Signals API also gives you "then". Every day's signals are automatically archived: yesterday, last week, last month — searchable any time. Why does history matter? First, trend review — a model topped HuggingFace last week; is the momentum continuing this week? Historical data shows trajectories, not isolated snapshots. Second, recovering what you missed — back from a week away, the archive fills in everything important you missed, so you never fall behind because you "didn't see it". Third, accumulating an asset — after three months, a year of continuous operation, this archive is a unique data asset documenting the full evolution of the AI industry over that period. For research and investing, history isn't nostalgia; it's the baseline of analysis. Without history, you can't tell whether "now" is normal fluctuation or an anomaly. The archive compounds daily — every day you delay subscribing is a day of data thickness you never get back.

🤖 Machine-readable · Is the data an asset?

Information products come in two kinds: for humans and for machines. Signals API does both — a visual dashboard for humans, plus a JSON API and CSV/JSON export for machines. Why is machine readability a necessity? Because in real workflows, "humans" shouldn't be the only consumer: quant teams feed signals into models, researchers import data into analysis tools, content teams batch-fetch topics, founders monitor competitors — all of these need structured data, not screenshots. Our API provides query endpoints filtered by company, topic, source, type and time, returning standard JSON with stable fields and uniform formatting, ready to plug into your code. History supports batch retrieval by date, and one-click export for offline analysis. For humans, a dashboard; for machines, an API; for workflows, exports. Human-readable and machine-readable — that's what makes it a true data asset.
